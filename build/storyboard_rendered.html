1. Hook / Context

Opening title card (Title, Concept, Student Name & ID) transitions into a tangled web of 3 coupled equations with glowing variable links, morphing into an organized upper-triangular staircase matrix. Pose the motivating question: naive substitution on 3+ variables becomes messy fast. Introduce Gaussian elimination as an algebraic + geometric machine that builds a clean staircase.
2. Concept Introduction
0:42 – 1:35
 Define notation, augmented matrices, the 3 elementary row operations, and Row Echelon Form (REF). Show Ax = b sliding smoothly into [A | b] with color-coded columns. Animate 3 operation cards (Swap, Scale, Replace) and highlight the echelon staircase + pivot rules. State the learning goal. Define coefficients, constants, pivots (leading nonzero entries), and why only Swap (Ri↔Rj), Scale (cRi, c≠0), and Replacement (Ri+kRj) are valid.
3. Visual Intuition
1:35 – 2:38
 Build geometric intuition first before 3×3 algebra: show why row ops preserve the solution set. 2D grid with x+y=5 (cyan) and 2x-y=1 (amber) intersecting at (2,3). Animate line pivoting smoothly around (2,3) into y=3 during R2←R2-2R1. Then rotate 3 shaded 3D planes in ℝ³. Explain: Swap relabels lines; Scale leaves line invariant; Replacement pivots the line around the intersection because L2+kL1=0 holds wherever both L1=0 and L2=0. Extend to 3D planes.

4. Worked 3×3 Example
2:38 – 4:15
 Demonstrate how & why every step works with all-integer arithmetic, pivots, REF, and back-substitution. Animate matrix transitions one step at a time: curved swap arc (R1↔R2), highlighted multiplier pills (R3-2R1, R3-3R2, -1/14 R3), glowing echelon staircase, then animated upward back-substitution cards. System: 2y+3z=7, x-y+2z=9, 2x+4y-z=-3. Swap to avoid 0 pivot. Eliminate col 1 & col 2. Scale R3 to z=3. Back-substitute: y=-1, x=2. Check in all 3 original equations.
5. Interpretation
4:15 – 4:58
 Connect final algebraic structure back to geometry and classify all 3 linear-system outcomes. 3-column visual comparison cards: (1) 3 pivots → 1 intersection point; (2) [0 0 0 | 5] contradiction → parallel/non-intersecting planes; (3) [0 0 0 | 0] free variable → planes meeting along a line. Explain what pivot count and the bottom row tell us: full pivots mean a unique point (2,-1,3); a 0=5 row means no solution; a 0=0 row leaves a free parameter and infinitely many solutions.
